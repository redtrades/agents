# Current historical synthesis

## Recovered throughline

“Genetic swarm” consistently meant improving reusable methods from evidence, not letting agents rewrite the organization. The current owner decision broadens that abstraction beyond prompt mutation to experiments in software methods, business ideas, operations, research, market signals, proposals, prompts, and workflows.

## What the evidence supports

- Separate the evolving genome from the execution substrate and knowledge record. Roles and capabilities are candidates for change; policy and promotion authority are not ordinary mutation targets.
- Every experiment needs a hypothesis, budget, metric, stopping rule, evidence, lineage, and proposed implication. A result appends evidence; it does not automatically rewrite the canon.
- Evaluation needs fixed and held-out cases, with an independent selector. Historical sources repeatedly identify self-review, unmeasured optimization, and drift as failure modes.
- Losing variants matter: retain evidence and lineages so the portfolio learns without repeatedly rediscovering failures.
- Promotion is a policy-governed act that moves validated learning into institutional knowledge. It is distinct from running an experiment and from infrastructure deployment.

## Historical caution

GEPA, DSPy, council, and swarm material documents options and hypotheses. Nothing here claims that a given method was behaviorally verified in this environment.
