# Current–historical synthesis

## Current owner decisions

Infrastructure includes runtimes, orchestration and workflow engines, storage, identity, networking, observability, sandboxes, and deployment. Individual workflows are versioned operational artifacts; skills, plugins, MCP servers, prompts, and models are capabilities. Material infrastructure must be selected through real-workflow bakeoffs and adopted only on evidence of net improvement after migration and operating cost.

## Historical evidence worth retaining

The archive records a recurring architecture boundary: a durable workflow engine can own execution history, waits, retries, and cancellation, while a separate authority owns task identity, admission, leases, budget, approvals, candidate identity, review, promotion, and reconciliation. Historical research documents several viable engine families and insists that persisted workflow state does not itself authorize an external effect.

Earlier OpenClaw material also separates declarative state, replaceable harnesses, model routing, and execution environments. It is useful lineage, but its named routing, container, scheduler, and observability components are not current selections.

## Implication for the archive

The retained asset is the evaluation boundary: one representative, fault-injected vertical slice, exact versions, lifecycle evidence, operating cost, and clean removal. A framework feature list, local installation, or a successful demo is not a selection result.
