## Objection A. "How do I know this is any good?"

This is now a harder question than the prior research assumed, because a good answer already exists
in this market and it belongs to somebody else.

### The block

> Straight answer first: I cannot show you what SAS-GPS can show you. They have been writing federal
> proposals since 2002, they carry an A+ BBB rating, and their testimonials have real names, titles
> and companies attached. Bob Martin at Michels. Phil Hawley at Ewing. I have none of that. Zero
> customer references, and I am not going to invent a substitute for them.
>
> If you want a firm with a bench of writers and a twenty-four year track record to run a large
> proposal, call them. I mean that.
>
> What I can show you is the thing itself, before you pay. Every file I have built is on the site in
> full. Not a redacted teaser, the whole document, gaps page included. Nobody else in this trade
> publishes their work product, because a testimonial is cheaper to produce and it never has to
> survive being checked.
>
> A testimonial tells you a customer was happy. The file tells you whether the numbers are right.
> Open one, pick any figure, click the link beside it. It should land on the federal record showing
> the same number with the same date. If one doesn't, email me and I will pull the file down and
> tell you what broke.

### Why it is written this way

- **It concedes the strongest version of the opposing case, by name, first.** `brand/voice.md`
  already establishes that conceding is the most credible move in this copy, and the buyer will find
  SAS-GPS whether or not we mention them. Being the one who told them costs a small number of sales
  and buys the rest of the page.
- **It refuses the fake substitute.** GTM §2.4 named references as the binding proof gap. Every shop
  in this segment fills that gap with an unverifiable aggregate. Federal Government Advisors carries
  4.9 stars across 167 Trustpilot reviews while holding 32 BBB complaints in the same window
  (competitor-pain §2.7). Saying "I have none" is the only sentence in this paragraph that cannot be
  faked.
- **The invitation is falsifiable.** "Click any number" is a fifteen-second test the reader can run
  and that we would fail publicly if we were sloppy.
- **The relationship-versus-artifact distinction is the actual argument.** competitor-pain §2.5 found
  two EZGovOpps reviewers rating the product 5.0 and 4.5 while calling the export unusable. The
  rating measured the relationship; the complaint measured the artifact. That is the whole objection
  in one observed pair.

### The short version, for a reply on a phone

> I have no references yet. What I have is every file I've built, published whole, before you pay.
> Pick any number in one and click it.

## Objection B. "Why you, rather than a proposal shop that has been doing this since 2002?"

The real objection, and the one the prior research never asked.

### The block

> Four differences, and only the first two are about them.
>
> **The price is on the page.** Every services provider on page one of the searches a small firm
> actually runs withholds it. SAS-GPS advertises "Transparent Pricing" and puts the number behind a
> seven-field form and a bid strategy call. Mine is $1,500 and you just read it.
>
> **You do not have to talk to me.** Their intake asks whether you have bid before and which
> solicitation you need help with. Mine needs your UEI, because your contracts are already public. No
> call, no questionnaire, nothing to upload.
>
> **The unit is one contract.** Not an engagement of unknown size that starts with a scoping call.
> One named contract, one file, one price, paid once, and then we are done unless you come back.
>
> **I will tell you not to bid, and I will show you the field that made me say it.** If the
> solicitation says full and open and the last award on that work drew one offer, the file says the
> record does not support a bid, and you do not pay. I publish how many times that has happened.
> Nobody who bills by the proposal can afford that sentence.
>
> Where they beat me: a bench of writers, twenty-four years, and customers who will take your call.
> On a large complex proposal that matters more than anything on my list.

### Why it is written this way

- **Four claims, all checkable today.** Published price against a gated one, UEI against a
  seven-field intake, one file against an open engagement, a refusal against no stated refusal. Those
  are exactly the four surviving edges competitive-assessment §9.2.1 identified, and no fifth one was
  invented to round out the list.
- **The refusal claim is stated in its narrow, defensible form.** The sibling assessment falsified
  the broad version: PrimeRFP publishes a refusal on thin coverage, and their SKILL.md instructs the
  model to state limitations. What their own showcase brief could not do was refuse when the data was
  present and contradicted the recommendation, and it told a buyer a prior award was single-bid so
  competitive dynamics might favor a challenge, over a $90 button, when the federal record on the
  same page showed the award was never competed at all (competitive-assessment §3.2). **The copy
  claims a gate, not an ethic.** Citation without a gate is a bibliography.
- **The refusal count is a number, not a promise.** competitive-assessment §8.3 makes the case: a
  competitor whose footer says "never say no bid" can copy a marketing line about telling you not to
  bid in an afternoon, and cannot copy a counter that goes up. Whether a buyer cares about that
  counter is untested. See REPORT §6.4.
- **The last paragraph gives the sale away.** GTM §8 found the no-sale is what produces the referral,
  and a page that claims to beat a twenty-four-year-old firm on every axis is not believable to
  somebody who has been sold to before.

## Objection C. "Why should I pay you rather than use the free SAM alerts?"

Demoted from a headline objection to a secondary one, because the sibling assessment shows the buyer
comparing us to services rather than to tools. Still needed: SAM alerts are free, authoritative, and
the honest floor of this market.

### The block

> Use the free alerts. Set them up this afternoon. They come from the source, GSA publishes the
> instructions itself, you do not need a UEI or a registration to turn them on, and no paid tool
> beats them on speed because every paid tool is a copy of them.
>
> An alert tells you a notice exists. It does not tell you the last award on that work drew one
> offer while the solicitation said full and open. It does not tell you what the office actually
> obligated the last three times, as opposed to the ceiling they published. It does not tell you that
> the contract underneath it has no option years left on the record, or which of the twenty
> requirements your own award history already covers and which fourteen it does not.
>
> That is the evening. The alert delivers the notice for free. The $1,500 buys the evening you would
> otherwise spend turning it into a decision.
>
> If you have the evening and you like doing the work, keep the $1,500 and set up the alerts. That is
> a real answer and it is the right one for plenty of firms.

### Variant for a buyer who names a software tool

> HigherGov is good, it is cheap, it will draft you a Sources Sought response in about five minutes,
> and the $500 tier now includes live analyst support. It needs an account, a linked profile, your
> documents uploaded, and you at the keyboard. I need your UEI. If you want to drive the tool, drive
> the tool.

## The objection nobody says out loud, and the answer that has to be structural

The real first thought is **"is this the SAM registration scam."** GSA publishes an industry bulletin
about misleading marketing and imposters, and BBB documents third parties charging for free federal
processes (GTM §5.5). An unsolicited, personalized, deadline-shaped email from an unknown sender
shares every surface feature of that pattern.

Copy cannot fix this. Sequence and public identity can.

1. The first email asks permission to **give** something free, not to sell something.
2. The free thing is the buyer's own public record read back to them, which no scam builds because it
   costs real compute and produces no fee.
3. The price is on the public page before they ask.
4. No urgency exists that the federal record did not already create. The end date is the end date.
5. The site carries what a real business carries: legal entity name, a phone number, a physical
   address, a stated founding year, and a named person who approves the files. SAS-GPS publishes all
   five and gets named in AI answers; we publish none of them and do not
   (competitive-assessment §10.3, Test B).

And one discipline: **never mention their SAM registration.** It is public, it is dated, it would hit
hard, and it is the scammer's opening line. Banned in `assets/01-cold-email.md` for that reason.
