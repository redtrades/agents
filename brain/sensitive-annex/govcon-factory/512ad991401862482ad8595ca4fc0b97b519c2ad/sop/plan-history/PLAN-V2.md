## Step 1 — Gap statement

*Completion test: does this name a precise, evidenced gap rather than a solution? Yes — no product, price, or tier appears below.*

The plan prices its flagship product at $5,000/year and justifies that number with an outcome claim: "one won recompete = $500K–$5M+ in contract value → ROI story is 100–1000x" (business plan §6). Every one of the three source documents is silent on the one number that claim actually requires — the probability that a customer's win rate changes because of this product, and by how much. Nothing else in the plan — the price, the tier ladder, the 35–45% Snapshot→Core conversion target, the choice of a $1–50M/5–75-employee segment — can be evaluated as right or wrong until this is resolved, because all of it inherits its logic from an answer the plan never actually gives.

## Step 2 — MECE candidate map

*Completion test: do the candidates partition the causal mechanism space without overlap, and does at least one fit the plan's own evidence better than others?*

What is the buyer actually paying for — partitioned by causal mechanism connecting the product to a rational price:

- **A. Time substitution.** Product replaces manual labor (5–15 hrs/week → under 1). Priced against the buyer's labor rate.
- **B. Win-rate lift.** Product improves capture decisions on contests the firm would have bid anyway. Priced against incremental contract value × probability of causing it. This requires a win-rate number. None exists in any document.
- **C. Coverage / avoided total miss.** Product prevents a firm from never finding out a recompete existed at all — not "wins more of what it already bids," but "bids more of what's actually winnable." Distinct from B: this is an expansion of the opportunity set, not an improvement in odds on a fixed set.
- **D. Reassurance / compliance-monitoring good.** Bought for peace of mind, independent of measurable time or win-rate change. Priced like insurance.
- **E. Status/convenience anchored to a substitute's price.** Buyer pays because it's cheaper than GovWin and signals "we have BD infrastructure," decoupled from provable value. Priced by comparison, not calculation.

The plan's own §2 "Pain ranked by willingness to pay" is discovery/coverage pain top to bottom — "what's coming up," "who's vulnerable," "what did I miss" — not "help me win the ones I already know about." That's candidates A and C. The plan's §6 pricing logic argues candidate B. **The plan's evidence supports A/C; its stated price logic argues B. It never reconciles the two.**

## Step 3 — Crux, in plain language

*Completion test: is this a single sentence a non-specialist could react to, stated before any option appears?*

**This business doesn't know whether it's selling fewer missed opportunities — a coverage product, priced like a research subscription against a labor rate — or more won contracts — a capture-outcome product, priced like a value-share against contract value — and it currently prices itself as the second while every piece of evidence it actually has supports only the first.**

This is Mike's question, answered: **time/coverage (A/C), not outcome (B).** The number that pick requires, and the number that would falsify it, come next.

## Step 4 — Options tied to levers

*Completion test: does each option name a concrete, different plan, not a variation on messaging alone?*

Given the crux resolves toward A/C, four MECE options across price level, pricing unit, segment width, and delivery model:

1. **Keep the $5,000 subscription, re-anchor its logic to time-savings, narrow the segment** to firms with a real, staffed BD/capture function (where 10 hrs/week of manual research is a costed labor expense, not stolen owner-time). Drop the ROI/win-rate claim from the pricing argument entirely; keep it only as color.
2. **Reprice down, go wide/high-volume**, competing directly against VetBiz's price band ($1,200–2,400/yr). Requires per-customer review time to approach zero, which contradicts the plan's own stated moat (founder judgment building the relevance dataset). Rejected below — it trades away the one asset that's actually defensible.
3. **Switch to a per-deliverable service** — the $750 Snapshot becomes the primary product, bought at the moment of a specific capture decision, not as an always-on subscription. This is the cleanest fit for an outcome-adjacent pitch, because the buyer is paying for one decision's input, not a promised aggregate win-rate lift — no unproven number required.
4. **Narrow segment + event-triggered outreach**, using the pipeline to identify firms facing an imminent, specific recompete decision, then selling into that moment rather than running a generic top-of-funnel subscription pitch.

## Step 5 — Recommendation and three stress tests

*Completion test: does the recommendation follow from the crux (not from convenience), and is each stress test something that returns a real answer inside weeks?*

**Recommendation: a hybrid of 1 and 3, not a single pure option.** Keep Core as a subscription, but re-anchor its price to time-savings math for a narrowed segment. Promote the Snapshot from "lead magnet" to the primary near-term revenue vehicle and validation instrument, since it doesn't require solving the unproven win-rate number at all — it's sold against a specific decision, priced against that decision's stakes, not against an aggregate ROI claim nobody can currently back. This is a real change in what the business *is* (see "What changed," below), not a pricing tweak.

**Stress tests, each answerable in weeks:**

1. **Time-savings test.** Ask 5–10 target-segment firms directly, in discovery calls: how many hours/week do you actually spend on manual capture research, and what do you value that time at? If reported time is well under 5 hrs/week or isn't perceived as a real cost, the labor-substitution price collapses and so does $5,000.
2. **VetBiz differentiation test.** Buy VetBiz's $149/mo tier, pull real output for one NAICS, compare directly against what the Market Snapshot template promises. If VetBiz already delivers incumbent/recompete depth, the wedge is gone regardless of which crux answer is chosen — this is the fastest, cheapest kill-test available and should run first.
3. **Snapshot willingness-to-pay test.** Sell 5 real $750 Snapshots via targeted outreach before building anything past Ingest/Detect. Near-zero conversion falsifies the entire funnel, including the unverified 35–45% Snapshot→Core figure, before a dollar goes into Synthesize/Review/Distribute.

## The rebuilt plan

**Buyer.** Narrower than the draft. Not "$1–50M revenue, 5–75 employees" generically — specifically firms large enough to have a *real, distinct* BD/capture function (a named BD Director or Capture Manager role, or an owner who has quantifiably delegated real weekly hours to this work), roughly the upper half of the plan's own stated band. Owner-operators at the small end of $1–50M, doing this in stolen hours with no costed labor, are VetBiz's segment, not this one — competing there means competing on price against a $49/mo tool, which this business cannot win and should not try to.

**Promise.** Unchanged in substance, restated honestly: a weekly briefing covering what's coming up for recompete, who's vulnerable, and what got awarded that you'd otherwise have missed — replacing 5–15 hrs/week of manual SAM.gov monitoring with a fraction of that. Not a promise that win rate improves; a promise that the firm sees, and can act on, opportunities it would otherwise miss or find too late to bid.

**Pricing, with logic that survives.** Keep $5,000/year for Core — but justify it on time-substitution arithmetic, not outcome ROI:

- Assume a costed BD/capture role at a narrowed-segment firm runs roughly $50–75/hr fully loaded (estimate, not sourced from any document — flag this to Mike as an assumption to validate in stress test 1).
- Plan's own stated range: 5–15 hrs/week of manual research; midpoint 10 hrs/week, 48 working weeks ≈ $24,000–$36,000/year of real labor cost currently spent on this task.
- Cutting that to under 1 hr/week frees roughly $21,600–$32,400/year of labor time.
- A buyer will not pay near 1:1 for a tool that reduces (not eliminates) their own oversight time; a defensible capture rate for an efficiency-sold tool is roughly 10–20% of quantified value freed (heuristic, not a citation) — **$2,160–$6,480/year.**
- **$5,000 sits inside that band for the narrowed segment.** It does not sit inside that band for a $1–2M/5-employee owner-operator, where the labor cost being displaced is uncosted and fuzzy. This is the actual answer to "what does $5,000 rest on": a narrowed buyer whose current cost of doing this manually, in real dollars, is close to what the tool now charges to remove most of it.

Drop the "$500K–$5M ROI" line from anywhere a price is being justified. Keep it, if anywhere, as a footnote — it's true as a statement about contract sizes, not as a claim this product causes those wins.

**Tiers.** Free Digest stays (top-of-funnel, costs nothing). **Snapshot ($750) is promoted from lead magnet to primary near-term product** — sold repeatedly, per decision, per NAICS, not just once as a funnel step; this is where the "outcome-adjacent" story is honest, because it's bought right before a specific capture/no-bid decision. Core ($5,000/yr) stays as the subscription layer, now honestly framed as coverage + time savings, sold to Snapshot buyers who want it always-on rather than re-purchased per decision. Pro ($8,500/yr) survives unchanged — its logic (multi-NAICS, priority, calls) was never dependent on the outcome-ROI question and doesn't need rebuilding.

**Path to first revenue.** Weeks 1–2: Ingest/Detect, unchanged. Weeks 1–3, in parallel, not sequential: run stress tests 1 and 2 (discovery calls + VetBiz product comparison) — cheap, fast, and each one alone can kill the plan before more is built. Weeks 3–4: sell 5 real Snapshots (stress test 3) using early pipeline output, manually assembled if needed — this is now the actual first-revenue milestone, not a step toward Core. Only after Snapshots are selling and the review-time pilot has a real number (see below) does Core's build (Synthesize automation, weekly cadence, review UI) get committed to.

**Unit economics, with founder time priced as a real per-subscriber cost.**

- LLM cost: ~$7–35/customer/year (established, unchanged, immaterial).
- Founder review time: ~4–6 min/customer/week × 48 weeks ≈ 3.2–4.8 hrs/customer/year. Priced at a plausible $150–250/hr solo-operator rate (not stated anywhere in the source documents, which treat founder time as free): **~$480–$1,200/customer/year at the low end, ~$800–$2,000/customer/year at the high end.** This is the cost the original plan's "90%+ margin" claim hides by not pricing the founder's own hour.
- Net contribution per Core customer, after LLM and founder-time opportunity cost: **$5,000 − ~$35 − ~$480 to $2,000 ≈ $2,965–$4,485/customer/year**, even after honestly costing Mike's own labor. Still strongly positive.
- At the review-time ceiling (unchanged: 45–58 customers, 4–6 min/customer against a 5 hr/week budget), Core revenue alone runs ~$225,000–$290,000/year, minus ~$1,750–2,000 LLM, minus ~$30,000 other opex (per the financial model's own Y2 figure), minus $24,000–$100,000 of founder-time opportunity cost depending on the hourly rate assumed — **net residual value of roughly $118,000–$194,000/year**, before whatever Snapshot revenue adds on top. That clears comfortably even at the pessimistic end. The narrowed segment doesn't threaten hitting this ceiling — the serviceable market (4,000–8,000 firms per the plan's own VetCert-derived estimate) only needs to yield ~50 well-fit customers, and narrowing to BD-staffed firms likely still leaves 1,500+ addressable targets, an order of magnitude more than needed.

**Customer ceiling.** Unchanged from the prior analysis: 45–58 subscribers, driven by review time, not market size or compute cost. Narrowing the segment doesn't move this number — it improves the odds each outreach converts and each customer's price holds, which is a conversion-rate lever, not a ceiling lever.

## What changed from the draft, and why

**Changed:**
- **Pricing justification** — from outcome/ROI (unsupported, no win-rate number exists) to time-substitution (arithmetic shown above, defensible for the right segment).
- **Segment** — narrowed from "$1–50M, 5–75 employees" generically to firms with a real, costed BD/capture function. Reason: the labor-substitution math that actually supports $5,000 only holds where the displaced labor is a real, costed expense, not owner-operator stolen time — and VetBiz already owns the cheap end of that spectrum.
- **Primary product** — from Core-subscription-first to Snapshot-first. Reason: the Snapshot's per-decision framing is honest under the time/coverage crux answer without requiring the unproven win-rate number Core's original pitch depended on, and it validates willingness-to-pay before the more expensive pipeline automation gets built.
- **Sequencing** — stress tests 1 and 2 now run in weeks 1–3, in parallel with Ingest/Detect, ahead of any Synthesize/Review build. Reason: both are cheap, fast, and each can independently kill the plan; building the expensive part of the pipeline before running either would be building before knowing if there's anything to build for.

**Kept:**
- Deterministic pipeline architecture, USASpending-first data sourcing, provenance-first generation, the relevance-dataset moat, the general five-stage build sequence, the Free Digest, the Pro tier, and the core insight that GovWin is priced and scoped for a different buyer. None of this depended on resolving the crux, and none of it is contradicted by resolving it the way this document does.

## Assumptions that would kill it — testable in weeks, not months

1. **Labor-rate assumption ($50–75/hr, 10 hrs/week displaced) is wrong for the real segment** — falsified by discovery calls (stress test 1) inside 2–3 weeks.
2. **VetBiz already delivers comparable depth** — falsified by a single $149/mo signup and output comparison (stress test 2), days not weeks.
3. **Nobody will pay $750 for a Snapshot before a subscription exists** — falsified by 5 real outreach-to-sale attempts (stress test 3), 2–3 weeks.
4. **Per-customer review time is materially above 6 minutes** — falsified by timing the first 5–10 real Snapshot/briefing reviews directly, starting week 3–4, not inferred from the KPI dashboard's flat weekly constant.
5. **SAM.gov's entity-data resale restriction is stricter than the USASpending-first mitigation assumes** — falsified (or confirmed) by a direct question to GSA/counsel about Data Use Agreement scope for API-key holders, answerable in days, not months, and cheap enough that there's no reason to defer it.

Any one of these failing kills the specific number it tests, not the whole business — but #1 and #2 together are the ones that determine whether $5,000 survives at all, and both are answerable before any further engineering spend.
