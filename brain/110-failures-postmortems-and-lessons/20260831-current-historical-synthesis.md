# Current–historical synthesis

## Recurrent mechanisms

- Local, unpushed, or moving work was treated as delivered evidence.
- Prompt instructions and broad roles were treated as authority or isolation.
- Reviews, checks, and receipts were accepted after the candidate changed, or were mistaken for whole-lifecycle proof.
- Stale claims, worktree sprawl, ambiguous handoffs, cancelled runs, and inconsistent projections created duplicate or non-resumable work.
- Historical systems often expanded research and controls before proving the smallest runnable lifecycle.

## Lessons that survive

Use one bounded outcome, explicit ownership, exact artifacts, deterministic checks before independent judgment, and a current-state reconciliation before claiming completion. Preserve failures as dated evidence; do not let a document or prompt retire a failure without an observable enforcement and falsifier.

## Archive caution

The current archive task is knowledge recovery, not restoration of old automation or enforcement. A historical “required prevention” is an implementation hypothesis until a future owner verifies it on the chosen system.
