# Historical evolution

OpenClaw postmortems identified silent assumptions, context fragmentation, cascading workarounds, unavailable capabilities, and multi-agent debate as recurring sources of error. They moved toward constrained parallel research, single-writer shared state, structured briefs, failure budgets, and escalation.

The later platform ledger translated many incidents into durable-candidate, principal-separation, generation-fencing, exact-review, and projection-reconciliation controls. The death-loop diagnosis then showed a meta-failure: recovery documents and legacy boards can themselves become a loop when they are not in the active authority path.

The combined lesson is modest: improve a bounded lifecycle only after locating the actual authority and proving the specific control. More documents, more roles, or more agents cannot repair a missing enforcement seam.
