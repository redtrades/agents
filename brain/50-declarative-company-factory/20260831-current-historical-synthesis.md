# Current historical synthesis

## Current reboot decision

Agents, swarms, projects, and companies should be declarative where useful, with imperative orchestration or business-process code where configuration is insufficient. A declarative company is both an organizational blueprint and a stateful operating unit. It shares the small organizational contract with typed domain extensions, while economic authority is semi-autonomous, capability-specific, risk-tiered, and never inherent in the declaration. Reuse is to be proven through portfolio vertical slices.

## Historical era-canon

The whole-estate reconstruction describes a solo operator directing goals, budgets, policy, and exceptions while a system can define, instantiate, operate, evaluate, improve, and retire companies. The OpenClaw lineage supplied durable concepts: portable mind/body/brain separation, bounded roles, durable evidence, and a distinction between operating a company and merely talking to agents.

## Durable factory contract

- A company has a goal, domain data and rules, workflows, roles, budgets, policies, evidence, and measurable outcomes.
- Domains own their specific inputs and outputs; shared organization concepts must remain small and replaceable.
- Experiments contain hypotheses, evidence, metrics, budgets, and stopping rules. They never rewrite canon merely because they ran.
- A delivery is a reviewable, evidence-grounded artifact, not a claim that an agent did work.
- The factory may manage bounded incubation, funding, evaluation, pausing, and retirement within owner caps; scaling and consequential commitments return to the owner.

## Boundary

This archive does not adopt a control plane, orchestration engine, runtime, memory service, or implementation plan. Those are candidates for later evidence-driven evaluation.
