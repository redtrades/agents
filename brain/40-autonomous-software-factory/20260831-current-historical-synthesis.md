# Current historical synthesis

## Recovered throughline

The factory intent is to turn a defined outcome into bounded work, artifacts, deterministic evidence, independent review, and a human-governed promotion decision. Historical sources agree more on these invariants than on any particular controller or market product.

## What the evidence supports

- The mature workflow shape separates planning, execution, deterministic gates, review, and promotion; code changes are not accepted just because an agent declares them done.
- Factory stages were explored as explicit transforms with contracts, gates, and artifacts. That is a workflow-artifact pattern, not proof that any prior runner is the right infrastructure.
- Git/issue/PR-oriented provenance was repeatedly proposed to make work resumable and attributable. The exact hosted forge or controller remains unresolved.
- Market research and self-hosted comparisons identify many candidate responsibility planes. They support a capability-driven, real-workflow bake-off rather than an immediate adoption.
- Software-factory and company-factory outcomes are co-equal current goals; shared reusable capabilities should be validated through vertical slices, not by building an abstract platform first.
- The long-term software-factory outcome is an engineering institution combining governed delivery, product creation, reusable capabilities, institutional learning, portfolio management, and retirement of failed products.

## Historical caution

Current `agent-platform` code, lifecycle rules, and reports are evidence about what was attempted and learned. They are not instructions for this archive or authorization to rebuild a controller.
