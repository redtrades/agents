## 1. Shipley methodology and APMP Body of Knowledge

Shipley Associates' own guidebooks were not directly fetchable in this session (paywalled/product pages); findings here are from secondary sources describing the Shipley methodology, cross-checked across two independent writeups plus the already-cited GovEagle Shipley guide in `sop/SOP-DELIVERABLES.md` §0.2/§5:

- **Seven-phase lifecycle**: market segmentation → long-term positioning → opportunity assessment (bid/no-bid) → capture/opportunity planning → proposal planning (kickoff, outline, storyboards) → proposal development (writing, reviews, compliance) → post-submittal (orals, discussions, negotiations). ([GovEagle Shipley process guide](https://www.goveagle.com/blog/complete-shipley-process-guide))
- **Color team reviews**: the structured internal-QC mechanism, run as independent checkpoints against Section L/M criteria, not author self-review:

| Team | Timing | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Blue | Pre-RFP / at release | Strategy validation, section assignments, win-theme seeding |
| Pink | ~65–70% draft | Content completeness, narrative coherence, compliance-matrix alignment |
| Red | ~90–95% draft | Independent evaluator-perspective scoring against Section M criteria; reviewers must not be the section's own writers |
| Green | Alongside/after Red | Price-to-technical-narrative consistency, competitiveness |
| Gold | Near-final | Executive sign-off |
| Black Hat | Capture phase | Competitive positioning/differentiation |
| White Team/Glove | Post-submission | Production QC, lessons learned |

([VisibleThread color-team guide](https://www.visiblethread.com/blog/color-team-review-guide/); [GovEagle](https://www.goveagle.com/blog/complete-shipley-process-guide)) Red Team is repeatedly described as carrying the most weight, and its documented failure modes are directly actionable for a QC checklist: strategy/compliance problems surfacing at Red Team instead of Blue Team (expensive to fix that late); reviewers who are the proposal's own writers (destroys independence); reviewers arriving without the compliance matrix, Section M criteria, and win themes in hand; unstructured feedback ("strengthen this") instead of specific strength/weakness/deficiency/risk findings scored against the government's own rating vocabulary. ([VisibleThread](https://www.visiblethread.com/blog/color-team-review-guide/))
- **Win themes**: customer-benefit-framed differentiators developed during capture (6–12 months pre-RFP per the secondary sourcing), which then become section narratives grounded in the customer's stated priorities/pain points, not generic capability marketing. ([GovEagle](https://www.goveagle.com/blog/complete-shipley-process-guide))
- **Compliance matrix**: the mechanism that translates every Section L instruction and every Section M evaluation factor into a trackable row with a named owner, explicitly the artifact that keeps writing from drifting off what's actually scored. ([GovEagle](https://www.goveagle.com/blog/complete-shipley-process-guide); corroborated independently at §2 below via FAR 15.305 and the VA Source Selection Guide.)

**APMP Body of Knowledge** (bok.apmp.org): organized as 51 topics across seven categories, each with an introduction, best-practices list, cross-environment application notes, and trends section; membership-gated for full text, so findings here are limited to what's publicly indexed. ([About the APMP BOK](https://bok.apmp.org/about-the-bok/)) Two APMP concepts directly useful to register guidance:

- **"Ghosting"**: highlighting a competitor's weakness or downplaying their strength *without naming them*; done by mapping competitor weaknesses to the customer's stated evaluation criteria and stating your own strength as the antidote ("guaranteed uptime" rather than "competitor X has reliability problems"). Explicitly warned against: naming competitors, false/unsupported claims, negative language that reads as unprofessional to an evaluator. ([APMP-NCA: How to Ghost the Competition](https://apmpnca.org/blog/how-to-ghost-the-competition/)) This is not directly relevant to the SDVOSB-set-aside proposal case this factory serves most (competitor identity is often not knowable pre-award and ghosting is more of a best-value/tradeoff-competition tactic), but it is directly relevant register guidance: **never name a competitor, never use unsupported negative language about anyone**, evidence every claim.
- Proposal style practices are explicitly described as governed by **customer-dictated standards first, in-house style guide second**, i.e., Section L formatting instructions always override a firm's own style preferences. (bok.apmp.org glossary/style-guidelines indexing, per search)

## 2. FAR Part 15 mechanics: Section L/M, evaluation, past performance, price realism

Primary-source FAR text, fetched directly:

- **Section L / Section M relationship** (FAR 15.204-1 Uniform Contract Format; [FAR Part 15](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/part-15)): Section L (Instructions, Conditions, and Notices to Offerors) tells offerors what and how to submit; Section M (Evaluation Factors for Award) is what the agency scores against. **FAR 15.305(a)** binds the agency to evaluate "solely on the factors and subfactors specified in the solicitation," nothing more, nothing less. ([FAR 15.305](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/15.305)) This is the regulatory anchor under the practitioner claim (§0.4, §1) that a compliance matrix mapping every Section L instruction to its Section M factor is the single most load-bearing internal-QC artifact a proposal team produces.
- **Past performance (FAR 15.305(a)(2))**: evaluated on recency, relevance, source credibility, and performance-quality trend; an offeror with no relevant history "may not be evaluated favorably or unfavorably" on the factor: absence is neutral, not disqualifying. ([FAR 15.305](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/15.305); corroborated at [ContractingHQ](https://kthq.org/past-performance.html))
- **Cost/price realism vs. reasonableness (FAR 15.404-1)**: price analysis (comparison to prior prices, IGE, published price lists) establishes a price is not *too high*; cost realism analysis, required on cost-reimbursement contracts and permitted on some fixed-price-incentive/exceptional cases, establishes costs are not unrealistically *low* relative to the offeror's own technical/staffing approach. ([FAR 15.404-1](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/15.404-1)) **Unbalanced pricing** (one or more line items materially over- or under-stated despite an acceptable total) is a distinct, separately-analyzed risk category: an offer *may* be rejected for it but agencies are not required to reject solely on that basis. ([Tillit Law: unbalanced pricing](https://tillitlawfirm.com/featured-insights/federal-procurement/unbalanced-pricing-associated-risks-in-federal-contracts))
- **Debriefing content (FAR 15.505 pre-award / FAR 15.506 post-award)**: a post-award debriefing must include the significant weaknesses/deficiencies found in the debriefed offeror's own proposal, both offerors' overall cost/price and technical ratings, past-performance information on the debriefed offeror, overall ranking if one was developed, and a summary of the award rationale, but **explicitly excludes point-by-point comparison** against the winning proposal, and excludes trade secrets, proprietary cost/profit breakdowns, and reference names. Request window: 3 days after award notice; agency target: debrief within 5 days of request. ([FAR 15.506](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/15.506)) This is the regulatory reason a losing offeror learns *that* something was weak but rarely the fine-grained *why-relative-to-the-winner*, relevant to how confidently the rubric can claim to know "what evaluators actually score" versus infer it from GAO decisions and agency-published source-selection guides instead.

## 3. VA Source Selection Guide (June 2017): the single most on-point primary source found

[va.gov/oal/docs/business/pps/guideVAsourceSelection.PDF](https://www.va.gov/oal/docs/business/pps/guideVAsourceSelection.PDF), the VA's own internal procedure for how its contracting officers run source selections. Given this factory's VA/SDVOSB concentration (`AGENTS.md`), this is the most directly applicable evaluator-side document found in this research, and it was fully readable (unlike the Federal Register document, §0.5). Key extracted content, with page numbers from the PDF:

- **Combined technical/risk adjectival ratings** (p.14–15, Table 1): Outstanding ("exceptional approach and understanding... strengths far outweigh any weaknesses... risk of unsuccessful performance is very low"), Good ("thorough approach... strengths which outweigh any weaknesses... risk... is low"), Acceptable ("adequate approach... strengths and weaknesses are offsetting or will have little or no impact... risk... no worse than moderate"), Marginal ("has not demonstrated an adequate approach... one or more weaknesses which are not offset by strengths... risk... is high"), Unacceptable ("does not meet requirements... contains one or more deficiencies. Proposal is not awardable").
- **Separate technical/risk methodology** (p.15, Tables 2–3) uses the same five labels for the technical rating alone (keyed strictly to strength/no-deficiency count) plus an independent Low/Moderate/High risk rating tied to schedule/cost/performance disruption potential.
- **Past performance has two separately-rated dimensions** (p.16–17): a four-level **relevancy** scale: Very Relevant (essentially the same scope/magnitude/complexity), Relevant (similar), Somewhat Relevant (some overlap), Not Relevant (little/none); and a five-level **performance confidence assessment**: Substantial, Satisfactory, Limited, No Confidence, or **Unknown Confidence (Neutral)** when the record is too sparse to rate meaningfully (p.18). This is the VA's own articulation of the FAR 15.305(a)(2) "no favorable-or-unfavorable" neutral-absence rule (§2 above), made concrete with a named rating label.
- **Small business/SDVOSB evaluation** (p.18): the VA's Veterans First Contracting Program (Pub. L. 109-461, implemented at VAAR 819.70, §0.3 above) requires set-asides for SDVOSB/VOSB to take precedence over other FAR Part 19 small-business preferences; when small-business participation is scored as its own factor/subfactor, agencies choose either a pass/fail (acceptable/unacceptable) rating or the full Table 1/2 adjectival scale.
- **Explicit caution against over-subdividing evaluation factors** (p.8, italicized in the source): "More evaluation factors are often a net negative... further diminish the importance of any one aspect of the factor and introduce unnecessary complexity... can also lead to closely rated proposals with little discrimination." Not proposal-writer-facing advice per se, but useful context for why a compliance matrix built against the *stated* Section M structure (not an inferred finer one) is the right level of granularity.
- **Debriefing procedure detail** (p.23–24, Attachment 2): confirms the FAR 15.505/15.506 mechanics in §2 with VA-specific pre-award-notice vs. post-award-notice content tables, and documents that the VA runs debriefing "dry runs" with sample offeror questions internally before conducting a live debriefing, evidence that agencies anticipate and prepare for the same weakness/deficiency questions this rubric is trying to help offerors avoid triggering in the first place.

## 4. GAO bid-protest sustain patterns: what agencies did wrong, inverted into what a proposal/evaluation must get right

FY2025 GAO Bid Protest Annual Report to Congress: 1,688 total cases; **14% sustain rate** among protests resolved on the merits (down from 16% in FY2024); GAO met the 100-day statutory decision deadline on all FY2025 final decisions. ([GAO-26-900695](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-900695)) The three most common sustain grounds, each with a fully-cited example decision:

| Ground | Case | What the agency actually did wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Unreasonable technical evaluation | *Emissary LLC*, B-422388.3, B-422388.4 (Jul. 29, 2025) | Credited the awardee for proposing 11 months of staffing when its proposal only covered 9; evaluators rated against what they assumed the proposal said, not what it actually said |
| Unreasonable cost/price evaluation | *KBR Services, LLC; Vectrus Systems Corp.*, B-422697 et al. (Oct. 4, 2024) | Agency unilaterally fabricated a "risk-adjusted price" to paper over a missing small-business subcontracting disclosure instead of reopening discussions |
| Unreasonable rejection of proposal | *SynergisT JV, LLC*, B-422384.2, B-422384.4 (Mar. 11, 2025) | Rejected a quote for using labor categories under SINs the solicitation didn't expressly prohibit; GAO required that exclusions be "specifically and expressly" stated to justify rejection |

(All three: [Government Contracts Legal Forum sustain-of-the-month roundup](https://www.governmentcontractslegalforum.com/2026/04/articles/government-contracts/january-2026-bid-protest-sustain-of-the-month-gao-sustains-protest-on-multiple-grounds-highlighting-unequal-treatment-of-offerors/); [Infrainsight FY2025 sustained-protest analysis](https://www.infrainsightblog.com/gaos-2025-bid-protest-annual-report-prevalent-reasons-for-sustained-bid-protests-and-how-to-avoid-them); [Snell & Wilmer top-three roundup](https://www.swlaw.com/publication/gao-reports-top-three-bid-protest-arguments-of-2025/))

A fourth decision adds the **unequal-treatment doctrine** directly: *Amentum Technology Inc.; SOS International LLC*, B-4238989 et al. (DIA, Jan. 27, 2026): GAO sustained because the agency assigned GDIT a strength for training content that was materially similar to, but not identical to, what it downgraded Amentum for; GAO explicitly rejected the argument that unequal treatment requires "substantively indistinguishable" language between competing proposals, and separately found DIA assigned SOS International a weakness for discussing "legacy networks... decommissioned over 8 years" that SOS International's proposal never actually mentioned, an unsupported factual finding with no basis in the record. ([Government Contracts Legal Forum](https://www.governmentcontractslegalforum.com/2026/04/articles/government-contracts/january-2026-bid-protest-sustain-of-the-month-gao-sustains-protest-on-multiple-grounds-highlighting-unequal-treatment-of-offerors/))

**Inverted into proposal-writing implications** (the point of this line of research per the task brief): the common thread across all four decisions is not proposal *eloquence*: it's **traceability**. Every sustain traces to the agency's inability to point to the actual proposal text supporting its rating. The practical corollary for how a proposal should be written: every claim that supports a strength should be stated in terms unambiguous enough that a rushed evaluator cannot misread staffing months, cannot conflate SIN categories, and cannot confuse one section's discussion with another's; precision of statement is itself a protest-defense feature, not merely a clarity nicety. A fifth 2025 decision worth noting for a different reason: *Mission Essential Group LLC*: GAO sustained a challenge to an LPTA methodology because the agency failed to adequately document why exceeding minimum requirements had no value, reinforcing (from the agency side) that LPTA vs. best-value tradeoff is a real, documented methodology choice disclosed in Section M, not an evaluator's ad hoc preference. ([Bradley: 5 most important bid protest decisions of 2025](https://www.bradley.com/insights/publications/2026/01/the-5-most-important-bid-protest-decisions-of-2025))

**Caveat carried over from `sop/SOP-DELIVERABLES.md` §1.1 (S7):** GAO's published-decision record is a lower bound: most dismissals and essentially all corrective-action-driven withdrawals never appear in the public docket, so absence of a published sustain does not mean absence of the underlying problem across the broader universe of protests.

## 5. Volume structure and the executive-summary question: verified, not assumed

Per the task brief's instruction to verify rather than assume whether Executive Summary is a separately-scored volume: **it is not, per every source checked.** Independent secondary sources converge: proposals are organized into four standard, separately-scored volumes: **Technical, Management, Past Performance, Cost/Price**, with Section L dictating exactly which volumes a given solicitation requires; the executive summary functions as a **framing document across those volumes**, not as its own Section-M-scored factor. ([OCIWins: Federal proposal sections and volumes](https://ociwins.com/proposal-strategy-execution/federal-proposal-sections-volumes/); independently corroborated by web-search synthesis across multiple GovCon-consultancy sources on this specific question) Consequently, `PROPOSAL-RUBRIC.md` treats Executive Summary as a short cross-cutting subsection, not a fifth scored-volume section, matching what the sources actually support rather than the four-volumes-plus-one structure the task brief flagged as a possibility to check before assuming.

Management-volume and price-volume specifics not otherwise sourced above: key-personnel resume/qualification requirements are a **named, common cause of an "unacceptable" rating** when a required minimum (years of experience, certification, clearance) isn't met ([Hinz Consulting: key personnel section](https://hinzconsulting.com/key-personnel-section/)); staffing-plan hours must reconcile between the technical/management narrative and the priced labor categories, since inconsistency between volumes is independently named as a disqualifier in §0.1 above and is exactly the kind of cross-volume consistency a compliance matrix and Green Team review (§1) exist to catch. ([OCIWins: management volume](https://ociwins.com/proposal-strategy-execution/proposal-management-volume/))
