# Current historical synthesis

## Recovered throughline

The durable intent was a provider-neutral workforce that can survive harness, model, and session changes. Agents are adapters and role blueprints, not the source of task identity, acceptance, or promotion. The current owner decision retains this aim while separating capabilities from infrastructure and policy.

## What the evidence supports

- A small baseline of persistent organizational roles plus on-demand specialists and bounded ephemeral workers was an early OpenClaw pattern. It was a topology proposal, not proof that a fixed agent count is optimal.
- Cross-harness portability was pursued through a common instruction and skill surface. The archive also records the failure mode: copied or over-layered instructions became unreadable, stale, or contradictory.
- Useful reusable capabilities are granular skills, model routing, research, coding, audit, handoff, and evaluation functions. Their availability should be scoped; discovering one does not require infrastructure adoption.
- Workflow artifacts carry continuity: issue/task records, handoffs, decisions, learning entries, evidence, and reviews. They are durable operating outputs, not runtime memory.
- Capability evolution must produce proposals and evidence. It cannot silently alter policy, canonical knowledge, or its own evaluation criteria.

## Historical caution

`agent-configs`, OpenClaw, and `agent-mesh` describe earlier operating surfaces and proposals. They are useful evidence of intent and failure modes, but have no governing force in this archive.
