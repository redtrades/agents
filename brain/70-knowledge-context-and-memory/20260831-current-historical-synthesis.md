# Current–historical synthesis

## Current owner decisions

- Promoted knowledge and exact source artifacts are authoritative; semantic memory and graphs are a working interface that must retain provenance, freshness, confidence, and scope.
- A global institutional pool is intended, with personal, project, and company overlays. Raw sensitive or organization-owned material remains scoped unless deliberately promoted.
- Git holds governed control and promoted knowledge. A larger knowledge service, if later justified, is replaceable. Proactivity is interest- and budget-bounded.

## Historical evidence worth retaining

The OpenClaw and agent-mesh material converges on a useful separation: immutable or append-only evidence at the bottom, derived semantic recall in the middle, and reviewed human-readable curation above it. It also records practical context-engineering lessons: a bounded always-loaded index, just-in-time bodies, dated verbatim source material, temporal supersession rather than deletion, and a search-before-synthesis discipline.

The historical five-tier design is valuable as an evidence model, not an adopted stack. Its “retain forever” language is superseded by the current tiered-retention decision: canonical and source artifacts are retained, while raw logs may be expired or compacted under sensitivity, value, legal-need, and domain-specific policy overlays. Its named stores and hooks remain hypotheses pending a one-store, provenance, privacy, and retrieval-quality decision.

## Stable boundary

Knowledge is distinct from infrastructure, capability definitions, experiments, and policy. A memory index may assist retrieval; it cannot become task authority, approval authority, or evidence provenance. A curated conclusion must still point to dated source bytes.
